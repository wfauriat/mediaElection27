Key findings

Outlet map: attention vs tone

Horizontal: tone slant, how much more favourably an outlet presents right-bloc candidates than left-bloc ones, compared with the average outlet (eligible candidates, centre excluded; whiskers = 90% interval). Vertical: coverage position from layer 1, where its attention sits. The two axes measure different things: an outlet can cover the right a lot and still present it negatively.

Press-average tone per politician

Net tone = (positive − negative) ÷ labels, averaged over outlets with enough labels (each outlet weighted equally). Descriptive: a politician going through a bad patch is presented negatively by everyone.

Relative tone by political bloc

An outlet's net tone toward a bloc minus the press average for that bloc. Orange = presented more favourably than elsewhere, blue = less. Values shown where the 90% interval excludes 0.

Who carries the tone

Of an outlet's negative (or positive) labels, the share carried by its own wording, by reported events, by quoted third parties, or by the politicians' own words.

Story mix

Share of each outlet's labels by main angle. Story selection (more scandal, more polls) is a form of slant distinct from wording.

Tone over time

Monthly net tone toward one politician: selected outlet vs the average of outlets. Months with fewer than 5 labels are blank.

Validation and method

    • Labels: Claude Haiku 5.5 (medium effort) with a written codebook; one request per article, headline + summary only, outlet name withheld. Fields: tone, voice, frame, salience, verbatim evidence.
    • Incidental mentions (named in passing) are excluded. Intervals: 400 bootstrap resamples of articles within each outlet, 90%; they hold the baseline fixed, so they are somewhat optimistic.
    • Descriptive only. Layer 3 compares outlets within the same weeks to separate editorial slant from the news itself.
    • Known limitations: headlines contradicted by their summary can be misread; voice is the least reliable field; keyword mention detection misses indirect references.
