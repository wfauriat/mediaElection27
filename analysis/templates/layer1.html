Key findings

Where each outlet's attention sits

Average lean of the candidates an outlet covers (hard-left −3 … hard-right +3), relative to the average outlet. Left of zero: attention tilted toward left-bloc candidates. This measures attention, not sympathy: an outlet investigating a camp also covers it a lot. Whiskers: 90% bootstrap interval.

Coverage by political bloc

Lift = the outlet's share of candidate coverage going to a bloc ÷ the average outlet's share. Values are shown only where the 90% interval excludes 1.0; hover any cell for details.

Share of voice over time

Weekly share of an outlet's politician coverage going to one politician, against the average of all outlets. Weeks where the outlet has fewer than 5 mentions are left blank.

Prominence

Share of mentions where the name is in the headline rather than only in the summary. Outlets without summaries are omitted.

Concentration

Effective number of candidates an outlet covers (1 ÷ Σ share² over the candidates). Lower = attention concentrated on a few.

Who appears together

Articles naming both politicians (all outlets). Hover for observed ÷ expected under independence.

Which outlets cover the race alike

Similarity of the politician mix (1 − Jensen–Shannon distance), outlets ordered by clustering.

Method and caveats

  • Unit: a distinct (article, politician) pair from the keyword extractor (exact or whole-word aliases, title + summary). Articles published from 2026-05-15. France Info's two feeds are merged.
  • Baselines weight every outlet equally, so BFM TV (about half of all articles) does not define "normal".
  • Intervals: 400 bootstrap resamples of articles within each outlet, 90%. They hold the baseline fixed and ignore week-to-week dependence, so they are somewhat optimistic. Cells expecting fewer than 5 pairs are greyed out as too thin to assess.
  • Feeds are samples of what each outlet publishes (section feeds vs front-page feeds), and keyword matching misses indirect references ("le leader insoumis").
